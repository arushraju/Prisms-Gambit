Prism's Gambit

I welcome you to my website Prism's Gambit, also known as The laser chess. Also, thank you of being here. This is my second full stack project where you can play a game of laser chess with a computer bot that I have built. This website is built using Javascript and Node.js (The web hosting service used is Render) and the bot itself is made in C language. The bot uses Alpha-Beta Minimax algorithm to get the best move in this game. To know how the website is built in detail, check out the link to the documentation below.

If you are new to the game and are not sure how to play it, you will also find a short instruction pop up below.

How to play?

Given below is detailed explantion of the game and also the about how can you play it on this website. For further clarity, a video explaining the game is also provided.

  • Rules of the Game

    • The game is played between two players: Red and Blue (similar to chess). Each turn follows a strict sequence:

      First, the player makes a move. Then, their laser is fired.

      After the laser finishes its path, the opponent takes their turn and fires their laser.

    • There are four types of pieces in the game (similar to Pawn, Bishop, Rook, and Knight in chess), along with a King. Unlike chess, there is no Queen in Laser Chess.

      On your turn, you may perform only one of the following actions:

      • Move a piece one square in any direction, or
      • Rotate a piece by 90°


      The King can move one unoccupied square in any direction but cannot be rotated.


      The Mirror can move one unoccupied square in any direction and can also be rotated.


      The Deflector can move one unoccupied square in any direction and can also be rotated.


      The Shield can move one unoccupied square in any direction and can also be rotated. Notice how shield could block the laser. Notice how shield could be destroyed by laser when hit by it in non reflecting side.

      This covers all possible movements and rotations in the game. Next, let’s look at the purpose and behavior of each piece.

    • King
      The objective of the game is to hit the opponent’s King with a laser.

      Any laser—Red or Blue—can destroy any King, including your own.

      If your King is hit by any laser, the game immediately ends and you lose.

      The King has no orientation—being hit from any direction destroys it.

      Mirror
      Mirrors are the heroes of the game. They allow players to create complex traps by reflecting incoming lasers at a 90° angle.

      However, Mirrors have a weakness: if a laser strikes a non-reflective side, the Mirror is destroyed.

      Be careful—your own laser can destroy your own pieces if you make a mistake.

      Deflector
      Deflectors are the immortal pieces on the board.

      They behave like Mirrors but have no non-reflective sides, meaning they cannot be destroyed.

      As shown in the diagram below, Deflectors can reflect lasers from both sides.

      Shield
      Shields can block a laser from one specific side.

      If a laser hits the Shield from either of the other two sides, the Shield is destroyed.

      The diagram below illustrates how a Shield blocks or gets destroyed by a laser.

  • How to Play Your Move

    To make a move, click on the piece you want to play.

    Once selected, all valid squares the piece can move to will be highlighted in red. Click on any red-highlighted square to move the piece.

    To rotate a piece, first click on the piece. A rotation button will then appear on the right side of the board. Press this button to rotate the piece by 90°.

    Note: In a single turn, you can either move a piece or rotate it — not both.
